## Premise and communication

An expedition has ten rounds to buy passage out of a collapsing mine. Ordinary players share an escape objective but can cooperate, withhold, steal, or eliminate people to lower the required fare. A hidden Smuggler has the abilities and appearance of their assigned public career but needs a private 25-coin passage.

No free chat, manual emotes, arbitrary messages, or editable signs are provided. Communication uses numeric contribution proposals, buy/sell offers, approve/reject controls, execution ballots, and structured Explorer nominations/election ballots. Planning, offers, proposals, and ballots can be submitted only while in a camp; selecting nearby targets to execute already prepared field actions is still permitted in the field. Contribution proposals are nonbinding promises, never automatic transfers. External voice/chat cannot be prevented; the rules ask participants to keep reward negotiations inside the game. Names and cosmetics lock at match start.

## Lobby, settings, and rematches

- Three to eight players join with a nickname and room code on phones or computers without accounts, installation, or a microphone. No microphone/camera permission is requested; optional game sound is unrelated to voice. One/two-player practice, if built later, is not a valid competitive match.
- Every seated player may edit permitted lobby settings. Each accepted change creates a new settings revision and clears all start votes. The first player has no privileged Start button.
- Every current player must explicitly vote Ready for the same settings revision, including an eight-player lobby. Three is the minimum. Joining, leaving, or a lobby disconnect clears votes; reconnecting requires voting again. A disconnected lobby seat is retained for 60 seconds before removal. No connected player can be silently excluded to manufacture unanimity.
- The server starts exactly once when roster validity, connection state, and unanimous votes hold together. A settings edit racing with the final vote is serialized against that revision.
- A completed match returns willing players to a fresh lobby. Readiness resets; everyone again has settings rights. New roles, randomness, wallets, map, and results belong to a new match ID. There is no owner migration problem because nobody has exclusive start authority.
- The initial settings surface permits specialist selection/random selection and cosmetic/accessibility preferences. Numeric balance presets are versioned by the developer; arbitrary prices or role counts are not live lobby edits. Shared editing can be disruptive; no host moderation feature is assumed.

## Guest identity, disconnects, and permanent forfeits

The server creates a private anonymous browser credential automatically. A nickname is a display label, never a password or seat-recovery key. Normalize names, require 2–18 visible characters, reject control/markup characters, and disallow case-insensitive duplicates in the lobby; lock names during the match. P1–P8 identifiers remain authoritative. A room code admits new seats only before start. After start, only an existing authorized seat may reconnect; changing a name or clearing browser storage cannot create a replacement seat in that match. The same browser can recover while its credential survives; cross-device/name-only recovery is not supported in the initial version. One active controller per seat, with generation fencing against old sockets.

**Confirmed one-round grace:** on server-detected disconnection, stop stale movement and record a deadline exactly 110 advancing base-game seconds later. The server uses heartbeat every five wall-clock seconds and marks the connection lost after ten seconds without a response, or immediately on a transport close. The grace begins when loss is detected; heartbeat detection can add up to ten seconds after a silent failure. Trials, succession meetings, and server/storage recovery pause base time and the grace; an individual disconnect, including everyone being offline, does not pause the match. Example: loss detected at round three second 60 expires at round four second 60, excluding pauses. A fully authenticated reconnect must be accepted strictly before the deadline; expiry wins at equality. Failed handshakes/pings do not renew it. A successful rejoin clears it; a later disconnection starts a new continuous absence. Intentional Leave uses this same grace, not immediate removal.

During grace the living avatar remains at its actual position, vulnerable under normal rules, counted in the fare/electorate and as an existing Explorer. Accepted durable theft/orders/actions survive only according to their ordinary phase/range rules; offline clients issue no new choices. No bot chooses purchases, attacks, or donations. Existing first-slot fallback behavior still applies; a fallback alone does not perform work.

At expiry, atomically mark permanent forfeit and revoke live-match authorization/tickets. A living unescaped avatar leaves one ordinary corpse holding its remaining wallet; inventory is destroyed, pending work/offers canceled, prior contributions retained, and the fare/electorate count falls by one. No killer credit or haunt target is invented; the forfeiting player receives no controlled ghost or later survival award. A dead ghost creates no second corpse or fare change. An already escaped winner keeps the recorded result but loses live controls/view. Deny live rejoin, including ghost/spectator controls; final result viewing and a fresh rematch are separate. Timeout processing precedes commands and escape snapshots at the same base-time boundary; a committed expiry cannot be undone by a late packet. If nobody living remains, finish the match rather than run empty rounds.

After a forfeit, if zero living unescaped non-forfeited Explorers remain, queue the normal willing-candidate election. If the post-arrival meeting has not started, use it; otherwise wait until the next round. No second election that round, no replacement for the loss of only one of two Explorers, and no automatic career assignment. A failed vote still retries next round. A deliberate sacrifice can lower the fare and leave loot after the delay; the loss of that player's award/work and the waiting period discourage it but cannot eliminate it under the chosen rule.

Retention defaults: disconnected lobby seats last 60 seconds (there is no round clock in the lobby); empty unstarted lobbies expire after 15 minutes. Keep active membership, private snapshots, receipts and forfeiture tombstones throughout the match and for 24 hours after it ends, then purge them and expire result access. Idle anonymous credentials expire after 24 hours unless attached to an active match; revoke them on cleanup. Redacted operational logs last seven days. Server-outage retention does not consume a player's game-time grace. Persist enough for exactly-once recovery; never clean up a live match because its clients are temporarily absent.

## Careers, appearance, and secret identities

All begin with two coins and three HP. Currency is integer-valued. Wallets, inventory, secret identity, and remaining actions are owner-private. Contributions and total fund are public. Exact HP is available to the owner and permitted nearby observers, never a distant live roster feed.

| Career | Ability and incentive | Starting maximum | Appearance |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Worker | Mine twice per round, one coin per action; other careers mine once. | 2 | Yellow hardhat, orange overalls, broad gloves. |
| Explorer | Only career that opens new rooms; one drill/action per discovery. Finder receives one coin from a treasure pile. | 2 | Tan field hat, teal coat, upright map pack. |
| Merchant | Sell extra actions and unlocked goods; earn limited commissions and retail margins. One personal healing potion each round. | 1 | Plum coat, cream collar, rectangular ledger bag. |
| Guard | Sell one wallet protection and one life protection service per round, each using a Guard action. | 1 | Cobalt uniform, ivory plates, square helmet. |
| Thief | Steal up to two coins per successful attempt; other careers take at most one. | 1 | Pointed charcoal hood with lime lining. |
| Scavenger | Loot in one second instead of three; can recover all remaining corpse coins, including the portion ordinary looters cannot take. | 1 | Rust jacket, rounded cap, large salvage pack. |

| Players | Roster |
|---:|---|
| 3 | Worker, Explorer, Merchant |
| 4 | Those three plus one of Guard/Thief/Scavenger; Guard is the default. |
| 5 | Two Workers, Explorer, Merchant, one specialist. |
| 6 | Two Workers, two Explorers, Merchant, one specialist. |
| 7 | Two Workers, two Explorers, Merchant, two distinct specialists. |
| 8 | Two Workers, two Explorers, Merchant, Guard, Thief, Scavenger. |

Assignment to people is random. In three/four-player games, use a 50/50 draw of zero or one Smuggler. In five-to-eight-player games, use a 50/50 draw of one or two. This resolves the earlier overlapping four-player ranges by treating four as the small roster. The number is secret. Each selected Smuggler retains a randomly assigned authentic career and all its talents; Explorer is not a preferred cover. Ordinary Thieves and Scavengers share the group objective. Suspecting and stealing from a Smuggler is a strategy, not an identity-based permission check or detector.

Use fixed P1–P8 identifiers, shapes, and cosmetic variants to distinguish duplicate careers. Same movement speed, collision footprint, reach, and selection area for everyone. No Smuggler visual tells. [Character specification](character-design-v01.md) defines the full cast and privacy rules.

## Round clock and allowed activity

**Confirmed timing:** 110 seconds, replacing the earlier 80-second draft. Ten full rounds would take 18 minutes 20 seconds, plus trials, replacement-Explorer meetings, and technical recovery pauses. One trial per round can add up to five minutes. A succession meeting adds 20 seconds only in rounds where it is needed. None of these wall-clock targets has been measured in a playable prototype.

| Logical time | Phase | Activity |
|---|---|---|
| 0–10 | Arrival grace | Finish walking into a camp, inspect inventory/map/catalog, and review a draft loadout. No teleport or new purchases. Movement is allowed, attacks are not. |
| 10–30 | Choices | Confirm prepared actions and purchases **while physically in a camp**. Purchases/service approvals close at 28; loadout edits and theft targeting close at 30. No field tool uses or attacks. |
| At 30 | Choice-close transaction | Finalize first-slot fallbacks; install paid Guard protections; then resolve queued thefts silently. |
| 30–40 | Departure grace | Walk toward field targets with the locked loadout. No purchases, tool uses, or attacks. |
| 40–90 | Field | Up to four prepared actions, including travel between nearby targets: explore, mine, loot, heal, light, contribute, or attack where legal. A trial pauses this clock for 30 seconds. |
| 90–105 | Return buffer | Walk to any discovered camp. No new physical actions or attacks; prepared contributions remain allowed. No new loot channels. |
| 105–110 | Settlement | Finish the return walk and prepared contributions. No shopping, looting, or other physical actions. |
| At 110 | Escape check | Freeze commands and evaluate natural escape first, then paid routes; end or begin the next round. |

The return buffer is a protected travel window, not automatic transport. Boundaries use server logical time and half-open intervals. A physical interaction must finish before field closes at 90; it cannot borrow the return buffer. Trials, succession meetings, and server recovery pause logical time; an individual disconnect does not. All weapon damage is blocked during arrival, choices, departure, return, settlement, and meetings. Ordinary attacks are also forbidden inside camps throughout field play.

Use 20 Hz integer logical ticks, 50 ms each. Server-received commands are assigned to the next unresolved tick at or after server receipt; round upward, never trust client timestamps or backdate. Gameplay durations and reconnect deadlines use the same grid. Three-second channels last 60 ticks. Round offsets are 200/560/600/800/1800/2100/2200 for seconds 10/28/30/40/90/105/110. A request assigned exactly to a cutoff is too late. A channel must complete on a tick strictly below 1800. Process overdue logical ticks in order so a delayed callback cannot invalidate a completion scheduled on an earlier tick.

Detect disconnect by existing heartbeat/close rules, record the detection tick, and expire at detection + 2200 advancing base ticks. Authentication must finish and resume be assigned a tick strictly before expiry; equality forfeits first. Trials/elections pause base ticks and use their own 20 Hz meeting clock; storage/runtime recovery pauses both. Individual disconnects do not pause either. Haunt receipts use the current base round even during a meeting. Recovery restores committed logical tick counters, receipts, claims and haunt allowances; it does not advance clocks by outage wall time.

**Manual camp return is confirmed.** Players remain at their actual position across round boundaries and must walk back to any discovered Safe Zone, including a forward camp opened by an Explorer. Camp and Safe Zone mean the same legal planning location. Each has a linked machine terminal. No automatic return, warp, automatic camp healing, or mine replenishment occurs. Returning late grants only the time left in the global choice window. Outside camp, purchases, loadout changes, nominations, ballots, proposal changes, and offer approvals are rejected. Previously committed choices remain committed if the player leaves camp early; an unsettled purchase still needs camp presence when it commits. Prepared field actions may select nearby targets when executed.

To keep the 20-second checkout short, show the catalog, current inventory, expiry, estimated next prices, and previous loadout read-only throughout the round. A player already in camp can draft their next cart/loadout before choices begin, without reserving stock, charging coins, or committing an action. At choices, one confirmation revalidates prices, availability, career, wallet, and quantities; it never silently accepts an increased price. Merchant standing offers and Guard offers can also be drafted in camp. No new planning controls are enabled outside camp. This lets players think ahead while keeping actual decisions and purchases at camps.

Four ordinary three-second channels need 12 seconds. The confirmed 50-second field window budgets up to 26 seconds of travel between targets and 12 seconds for targeting/latency/attention, then a separate 15-second walk home. Departure grace can cover some outward travel; settlement and next arrival provide recovery slack, not promised extra work. Use an initial speed of three tiles/second, adjacent interaction anchors no more than six walking tiles apart, and a 36-tile return budget after reserving three seconds of the return window. Include door/ladder travel and in-room paths; validate templates and measure real phone input. Four widely separated targets are not guaranteed feasible. Show a shortest-known-path return estimate to the nearest camp and warn when it exceeds the remaining return budget; do not open a field task with a misleading guaranteed-completion label. New camps reduce return distance. Deep exploration may require leaving earlier or sacrificing a fourth action. Before content is drawn, a new room whose interaction anchor reaches 36 walking tiles from the nearest discovered camp becomes a Safe Zone; replace its resource draw, do not combine a productive mine with a camp. Apply to branches and downward routes using actual path distance. Opening guaranteed mines must fit near the starting camp and take priority. This bounds camp spacing; it does not guarantee four arbitrary distant tasks fit. See the exploration model for generation and paid compass contracts.

## Action slots, items, and purchases

Each player receives exactly two free action slots per round; each additional slot requires a coin purchase. The Merchant can sell up to two extras per person, so four is the maximum. Movement, inspection, and camp-only proposals use no action. Every tool use, contribution, attack, loot claim, potion use, lantern placement, compass Survey, or trial initiation uses a slot. A kill is the consequence of a damaging attack; there is no free kill command.

Purchasing, selling, manufacturing Merchant stock, unlocking a catalog tier, paying a weapon surcharge, collecting a grant and preparing a loadout consume **zero actions**. Normal coin charges, career/camp/phase checks and stock limits still apply. Guard customers spend no action on the purchase; the Guard spends their prepared action and kit when delivering each service. Using a tool/item, attacking, looting, Surveying or initiating a trial still consumes the corresponding prepared action. **Deposit takes one action for any positive affordable amount, with no coin fee.** Two free actions, at most two paid extras, four total remain unchanged.

Each free slot includes at most one newly issued basic tool during choices; the first slot retains its entitlement until chosen or filled by fallback. This subsidy is necessary because charging a coin for a pickaxe that earns one coin would erase production income. Additional basic tools cost one coin at the machine. All players can get pickaxes, hooks, and crowbars; drills require Explorer; protection kits require Guard. Deposit and Trial use free prepared action markers in any owned slot, not paid physical tools. Both markers expire at round end and cannot be stockpiled/traded. Preparing Deposit never charges a tool or contribution fee, even in a purchased slot; buying that additional slot still costs its normal price. Trial’s round-adjusted fee is paid only when a valid trial is initiated. Paid equipment is separate. A stored tool can be equipped in a current slot; owning it does not grant an action. A purchased extra slot can use a stored tool or a separately bought tool. Free tool allowances do not carry forward, though issued tools survive until their expiry. Tools cannot be resold, gifted, or converted to coins. Workers can save unused issued pickaxes for later camp loadouts until expiry: a round-one pickaxe remains usable in round two, then expires at round-three arrival. Unused action slots do not carry over. Saving an item never grants a free later action or raises the production cap; a tool already consumed by an interrupted use cannot be saved.

During choices select action types; choose physical targets later, except theft and agreed contracts. No swapping after choices close. The first blank free slot reserves the career preset: Worker pickaxe, Explorer drill, Merchant crowbar, Guard wallet kit, Thief hook, Scavenger crowbar. It uses the remaining first-slot free-tool allowance and never performs work automatically. A blank second or purchased slot is lost. The capacity and delayed-collection rules below apply.

Every player has **seven total carried physical-item slots** for the entire match. Capacity is not upgradeable or a lobby setting. One item instance occupies one slot; no stacking. Equipped tools/weapons/potions remain in those same seven slots, referenced by the action bar. Equipping never creates capacity. A physical item may bind to at most one unspent action; spending it frees its inventory slot immediately. Money, action entitlements, Deposit/Trial markers, installed protection and deployed lanterns are not carried items.

Free-tool issuance, paid items, cache rewards, fallback tools, bound personal potions and withdrawals from Merchant retail stock all obey this limit. Capacity is checked within each atomic transaction; simultaneous grants cannot produce an eighth carried item. No overflow pouch, extra equipment storage, automatic destruction of an existing item or inventory upgrade exists. Expiry is processed before arrival grants. Expiry and item IDs survive equip, sale and reconnect.

Camp-only discard is free and destroys the selected item without coins, a ground drop or a gift. It is available outside meetings even after choices close; discarding a tool bound to a locked action forfeits that action. Discard never unlocks replacement selection. A kit/action already committed to a paid Guard service cannot be discarded, reassigned or canceled by the Guard before delivery; authoritative cancellation follows the service contract. This prevents inventory management from breaking a paid protection promise. A cache reward that cannot fit becomes the existing persistent ground pickup; claiming it requires Loot and capacity at start and completion. Coin loot needs no inventory slot. Evaluate start capacity after any tool consumed by that same accepted transaction: seven items minus one used crowbar leaves room for a cache pickup. An Explore similarly frees its drill slot before a cache grant. Recheck capacity on completion. A cache claim reserves its source, not future inventory capacity.

First-slot fallback still reserves the exact career preset and action at choice close. If the player is in camp with room, deliver and equip it once. Otherwise the entitlement waits at any linked camp terminal until that round ends. The player may free space at camp to collect it, including after choice close; no remote delivery, substitution, extra free grant or eighth item. Its lifetime counts from the issue round, not collection. A full-inventory warning identifies the pending preset. Unclaimed fallback/action expires at round end.

The Merchant's personal potions count toward the seven slots, with at most three unexpired personal potions including any pending grant. One bound potion is granted at arrival after expiry. If full, its single claim waits at camp for the current round, then expires unclaimed; collection requires capacity and preserves arrival-based TTL3. Claiming after choices close only stores it: no new action or loadout swap. Ordinary accumulation is possible by keeping potions in the seven slots. There is no separate carried potion pouch.

Retail stock is a separate bounded terminal warehouse (two manufactured per product per round, original TTL), not carried personal inventory. It cannot receive deposited personal items or be equipped/used directly. A legal retail delivery or Merchant self-withdrawal must atomically move an item into a free personal slot at camp before purchase cutoff; apply existing self-use eligibility and no-profit rules. Wholesale was paid on manufacture; do not charge it a second time on withdrawal. Unsold Compass stock still cannot be used by the Merchant. Pending automatic grants and warehouse stock provide no field-accessible storage.

Expiry is round-based: an item issued in round r with lifetime L is usable through r+L−1 and disappears at the next arrival boundary. Match pauses and trial seconds do not age it. Equipping, trading through Merchant sales, or deploying it never resets expiry. Basic tools last two rounds; weapons, lanterns, retail potions, compasses, and personal Merchant potions last three. Installed Guard protection and action slots last the current round. Inventory records individual instances and expiry, not just aggregate quantities.

Basic physical channels take three seconds; Scavenger Loot takes one. Only one physical channel may run per player.

Physical channels require a stationary actor. Turning is allowed while the required target remains visible and valid. Recheck eligibility every simulation tick; actual movement, room change, loss of target/range/LOS, new incapacitation, death, manual cancel, attack by the actor or field cutoff cancels committed work. The prepared self-Rescue exception remains valid while trapped. Any unblocked HP damage cancels a channel, even if the actor survives. A fully shielded hit does not. Invalid starts consume nothing; accepted starts consume the action and item, including when subsequently interrupted. Deposit is instant and can use its own prepared action during a channel.

Meetings freeze channel progress. Movement during a meeting marks a channel interrupted even if the actor returns to the same point before resumption. On resume, also recheck survival, target visibility, range and eligibility; a canceled channel never resumes or refunds. Turning alone remains subject to visibility. This prevents the move-away-and-back loophole without preventing movement during meetings.

Interaction defaults: drink a potion, use a Rescue kit, or place a lantern each requires one prepared action/item and a three-second channel during field time. Potions heal the user only. Rescue may target the user or a living incapacitated player in the same room within one tile; a trapped player may use a prepared self-Rescue despite the normal physical-action restriction. Clearing incapacitation happens on successful completion, not on start. An invalid healthy rescue target spends nothing. Lantern placement needs a visible, unoccupied valid floor point within one tile. Non-self physical targets must be in the actor's server-computed visible set as well as within reach/line of sight; knowing an ID or a shared map location is insufficient. The camp theft list and remote Deposit are explicitly separate targeting rules. Unavailable-target errors must not distinguish an unseen entity from a nonexistent one.

Resolve valid attacks before physical-channel completions in the same authoritative tick. A lethal hit cancels the victim's unfinished healing/rescue; no same-tick potion revives the dead. Only a surviving actor whose channel was not interrupted may complete it afterward; unblocked nonlethal damage cancels, while a fully shielded hit does not. Interruption consumes the committed item/action without applying healing, rescue, or lantern placement. These are selected execution defaults consistent with the existing three-second channel and tick order; they have not been playtested.


## Mines, treasure, and exploration

Everyone begins in the central underground Safe Zone, which is not a productive coin mine. Only Explorers drill new rooms. Existing rooms and ladders are traversable by everyone. A horizontal wall leads to a room at the same depth; downward excavation changes depth. New upward excavation is prohibited, but players can climb already discovered ladders back to camp.

The first completed discovery in the match is a mine at three/four players; the first two completed discoveries are mines at five-to-eight. Unused opening guarantees carry forward until consumed, including by an elected replacement; failed/canceled drills consume no guarantee. They are never replenished by an election. Thus using the available Explorer slots on discovery guarantees at least one mine and provides two for a two-Worker roster. The guarantee depends on those actions actually completing. Newly opened mines become active immediately when discovery completes, including round one. Workers may prepare Pickaxe/Mine slots before a mine exists and select the newly revealed mine as their field target; no field loadout swap, tool purchase or extra action is granted. If no living, unescaped Explorer remains, the survivors can nominate and elect a replacement at camp. This offers a way to restore discovery after a successful election, but does not refund missed rounds, revive anybody, create coins/mines, or reveal secret identities. The election procedure is specified below. The strategic goal is to protect Explorers; this is an incentive, not a guarantee that a selfish player could never benefit from attacking one.

Each mine has eight coins total, with at most two extracted per round. One miner per mine per round; one mine per person per round; Worker maximum two coins, every other career maximum one. Each coin needs a pickaxe/action. Reservations favor Workers, then rotating seat priority for simultaneous requests. Existing-mine no-show holds expire at round second 55; a mine discovered at base second d uses min(d+15,90), fixed once for that mine/round. This is a no-show deadline, not a wait: a reserved miner can start immediately. Selecting/reserving a revealed mine in the field is prepared-target selection, not a new loadout decision. A later request does not preempt a valid existing hold. Releasing/re-requesting cannot extend the deadline; after expiry, only a valid nearby channel start claims the mine, rather than renewed remote holds. A valid channel already underway retains its claim, but must finish before field second 90. After the first successful extraction, that mine remains locked to that miner for the round, even if they die. No automatic replenishment. Buying extra slots cannot bypass production limits.

Treasure contains a finite pile scaled by starting roster: 3 players 5–7, 4/5 players 7–9, 6 players 3–5, 7/8 players 4–6, sampled uniformly. The discoverer receives one coin from that pile; one separate Loot action collects its remaining chest coins. Chest looting is not subject to the corpse fraction. Deaths never rescale piles or generate more loot. These larger piles compensate for the newer coin sinks and unequal Explorer counts; they require playtesting.

The [exploration model](exploration-model-v04.md) specifies per-Explorer Markov memory, mine/treasure/Safe Zone/cache/trap/gallery weights, finite resources, room variants, and the rare natural route. A lucky streak raises trap probability from 2% up to at most 6%. Opening a room once fixes its result permanently; reconnecting or approaching another door cannot reroll it.

Traps deal no damage. A normal trap prevents physical work and field movement in the following round, but never teleports its finder. The current round otherwise continues, including walking back to camp. During arrival, choices, departure, return, settlement, and meetings an incapacitated player may walk; they may shop only after reaching camp and may use a prepared remote Deposit. They cannot steal or deliver Guard services while incapacitated. The status clears at the end of the affected round. A round-ten trap prevents further field work immediately but still permits the designated return walk and a prepared Deposit. A prepared Rescue kit is the one physical-action exception for an incapacitated actor: a three-second self-rescue during field clears the active status on completion. Other physical work and field movement stay blocked until that completes. Another player can also rescue them. A kit does not preemptively clear a trap scheduled for a future round. The resource analysis models only lost work and permitted deposits, not these spatial return rules.

## Replacing a lost Explorer

When there are zero **living, unescaped Explorers**, schedule a replacement election before the next normal choices window, after arrival grace. An Explorer temporarily disconnected within the 110-second grace or trapped still counts; a permanent forfeit does not. Expiry of the last Explorer queues this election without duplicating a surviving Explorer. One remaining Explorer is enough, even if the starting roster had two. A private departure can also create a vacancy; this check uses visible career/status, never hidden identity.

Selected procedure: one free 20-second succession meeting per affected round, with ten seconds for nominations and candidate acceptance, then ten seconds for a one-choice ballot among accepted candidates. The base round clock pauses for this meeting, preserving the full normal choices window. All economic/field actions and attacks pause; movement to camp remains possible. Only living, unescaped players can nominate, accept, or vote, and each must be in a camp when doing so. Nominees must consent and remain eligible in camp at close. Ballots offer any accepted candidate; a voter chooses one candidate or abstains. Freeze the electorate at meeting start to all living unescaped non-forfeited players. Election requires strictly more than half of that electorate, including those who abstain or remain disconnected within grace; ties or no majority appoint nobody. Try again next round if the vacancy remains. With only one living candidate, that player may nominate/accept/vote for themselves. No fee, action cost, automatic appointment, or role/coin refund is attached to succession.

**Confirmed role effect:** permanent conversion to Explorer. The previous career is replaced; this does not add a second career. The winner’s public outfit and career badge change at camp before the full normal choices window starts. Keep player identifier, HP, wallet, contributions, ordinary owned items, secret Smuggler status, and existing map/route progress. Start their own discovery history at state zero. They gain Explorer drilling/finder abilities and lose future privileges of the old career and cancel its uncommitted standing offers: Worker double production, Merchant commissions/potion grants/unlock control, Guard services, Thief bonus, or Scavenger full-corpse loot as applicable. Items restricted to the old career cannot be equipped; previously manufactured general goods and existing paid protections do not vanish merely because a career changed. The normal two free slots/tool entitlement provide drills; election grants no extra actions or free rooms. Unclaimed old-career first-slot fallback updates to the new preset only before its first delivery.

Protecting Explorers avoids losing discovery time and another useful profession. Election is a costly fallback in time and workforce, not an incentive to kill them. It does not prove every selfish payoff makes their survival optimal. If nomination/majority fails, discovery can remain unavailable; that is a coordination failure rather than an automatic replacement mechanic.

## Visibility and Safe Zones

Proposed vision uses a facing cone of 120 degrees plus a 0.75-tile immediate vicinity. Initial maximum distances are two tiles in darkness, four in dim light, six in bright light. Server raycasts clip the cone to walls and occluders; facing/turn speed is validated rather than trusted. These dimensions need mobile and movement testing.

A lantern costs two coins from the machine, needs one placement action, and brightens its room until its original three-round expiry. It does not bypass wall occlusion or turn an ordinary room into a legal Safe Zone. Bright mineral chambers are permanently lit Safe Zones. Their larger geometry still respects walls and cone direction. Meeting/shop/vote eligibility is a room property, not a light threshold.

The discovered map is shared. Live positions, health, body details, and action effects are sent only to permitted nearby viewers. Known rooms do not make their current occupants globally visible. The camp theft target list is an explicit exception: while its owner is in camp it reveals names of living players in that same camp even when a local wall clips sight, but no positions, balances, health, or tools. It does not offer a new target list outside camp. The client must not receive hidden state and merely hide it with a shader.

Safe Zones prohibit ordinary weapon attacks permanently. Trials are the sole execution exception. Stealing is allowed in safe and unsafe rooms. Camps grant no passive coins and contain no productive mine. An Explorer may legitimately drill an unopened boundary from inside camp during field time; prohibiting this would block the first discovery. That is useful exploration, not idle camping. Deeper expansion still requires reaching new boundaries. A camper can still contribute, trade, protect, and vote, so useful support work is not automatically idling. Camping reduces field production and discovery while the deadline advances. There is no invented camping penalty or passive camp income; trials and theft remain available. Whether these opportunity costs are sufficient needs playtesting.

## Silent theft

During choices, a player with a prepared Hook chooses another player from the same-camp list; self-theft is invalid. Selection, cancellation, or retargeting requires the attacker to be in camp; the previous outside-camp targeting exception is removed. Both must be alive, unescaped, and in that camp when selected, and still in the same room as one another when the intention resolves at second 30. If both walk into the same unsafe room before choice close, the prepared theft may resolve there; it cannot be newly targeted there. This preserves same-room theft without permitting planning outside camps. Players can cancel/retarget before close without spending or duplicating the action. One committed attempt per attacker/target per round; extra Hooks need different targets.

At choice close, install wallet protection first. Freeze opening wallets after purchases/protection fees. Resolve valid location intents in rotating seat order; cap each debit by that victim's remaining opening balance. Incoming stolen coins cannot be re-stolen in the same batch. Transfers are atomic and conserved. Each location-valid committed attempt spends its action/Hook even if the wallet is empty or protected; otherwise this could become a balance/protection oracle. A location-invalid intent takes no coins and consumes no tool, but its locked slot cannot be repurposed after close.

Ordinary attempts take up to one coin; Thief attempts up to two. No theft from the fund, corpses, escaped/dead players, or other rooms. Disconnection is not absence from the map: a disconnected living avatar remains a valid victim if physically co-located. An already accepted theft intention also persists if its attacker disconnects; disconnecting never grants immunity or refunds a commitment. Reconnection invalidates stale unaccepted inputs, not a durable accepted intention. No victim/global toast, visible animation, sound, identifying event, or public theft ledger. Each owner sees their wallet update silently; a victim can notice a change or infer suspects, which the game cannot prevent. The thief receives only their own transfer result, not the reason for a zero result. Results never disclose victim–thief pairings, including after the match.

## Merchant and machine

Each Safe Zone machine offers basic tools, weapons, and lanterns privately. Purchases do not create global receipt events. The Merchant's catalog tier is public; upgrade payments come from the Merchant's private wallet and burn coins. Skipping upgrades can attract suspicion without proving secret identity.

Base Merchant service: extra action slots. The first completed sale each round costs one coin, the next two, then three, and so on across all buyers; reset next round. At most two extras per person. Customers submit quantity, maximum price, and intended tools; Merchant approves/rejects or publishes a standing approval for that choices window. At second 28, rotate seat priority, fill one approved slot per buyer before second slots, and charge only an affordable quote within the cap. Buyer and Merchant must both be in camps when the sale commits; leaving camp suspends pending purchases. Delivered slots survive the Merchant's later death. If the Merchant is dead or changes career, no new sales/upgrades; unlocked tiers remain recorded and the basic machine still works.

Each approved extra-action bundle must be accepted **before tick 560 / second 28** and settled at tick 560. Bind exactly one eligible owned item ID, a free Deposit/Trial marker, or an explicit paid tool/item line. The item must be eligible for the action; one owned item cannot back multiple bundles or actions. An extra slot never includes a new free tool.

The order records its maximum slot price, agreed item price (zero for an owned item/marker), any unpaid stored-weapon surcharge top-up, and maximum combined total. Top-ups already paid are not charged again; an unpaid top-up commits only with the successful bundle. A newly purchased basic tool costs one coin. At settlement recheck authority, career, round, both parties' camp presence, stock, owned-item eligibility, seven-slot capacity for a new item, wallet and all price caps. Use rotating seat order, one slot per buyer before second slots. Commit the slot, dependent item delivery/reservation, all coin movements and the completed-sale counter together. Failure commits none of that bundle and does not advance the sale counter. Nothing may consume an action for checkout.

An order for two extras contains two independently priced bundles; the first can succeed while the second fails. Show each result and quote explicitly, never charge an unusable slot. No late discretionary tool purchase is allowed after 28. An owned item or marker needs no new inventory capacity. Example: a player with two coins and a free inventory slot buys the first extra slot for one plus a drill for one; both arrive or neither does. A full inventory fails that bundle without charging; a bundle using an already-owned drill can still succeed.

**Selected Merchant profit control:** at most two coins of trade profit per round, combined across extra-action commissions and retail margins. Each external extra-action sale pays the Merchant up to one coin, limited by the remaining profit allowance; the rest of the buyer's quoted price is burned by the machine. The price still rises 1, 2, 3, … even after the profit allowance is exhausted. Merchant self-purchases burn the entire quoted price and advance the same sale counter without creating profit. Selling does not use a field action.

For a retail good costing C to manufacture and selling for P, pay the Merchant C plus min(P−C, remaining profit allowance), and burn the rest of P. Reimbursement of the already-paid wholesale cost is not profit. Allocate the shared two-coin allowance in deterministic committed-sale order, never twice on retry. The private Merchant UI shows profit remaining and quotes; wallets remain private. The public profile shows contributed coins, unlocked catalog tier, and cumulative coins invested in upgrades, but no secret-role accusation or automatic suspicion score.

Example: extra-action sales at 1, 2, and 3 coins cost buyers six total. Merchant receives two total; the machine burns four. Pure trading can therefore earn at most 20 over ten rounds, plus two starting coins; purchasing all upgrades burns 15, leaving seven before other earnings or spending. This bounds trade income, not total wealth: mining, chest loot, theft, and contributions still matter. A Merchant who unlocks nothing and contributes little may be suspected, including when secretly a Smuggler, but poor demand or cash shortages are also possible. The game does not treat this as proof. The [new policy-dependent calculation](defaults-calculation-v05.md) models purchase burns and capped profit. It supports this initial limit but does not validate full strategic demand, movement, or faction balance; those still need the rules engine and playtests.

Selected catalog progression, one upgrade per choices phase, only if affordable:

| Tier | Unlock burn / earliest round | Stock | Wholesale → fixed retail |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | Available initially | Extra action market | Escalating service price above |
| 1 | 3 / round 2 | Healing potion: heal one HP, one action, never above three. | 1 → 2 coins |
| 1 | Included in the same tier-1 upgrade | Explorer compass: one three-second Survey toward the nearest real opening; single-use, three-round inventory lifetime. | 8 → 9 coins |
| 2 | 5 / round 4 | Rescue kit: release one currently trapped nearby player, including self, one action. | 1 → 2 coins |
| 3 | 7 / round 6 | Reinforced lantern: same lighting as a lantern, five-round lifetime from manufacture. | 2 → 3 coins |

Tier order is sequential. Stock purchases are private; tier unlocks public. Retail goods are stocked before being sold, never minted by payment. At most two units of each unlocked product can be manufactured per round; leftover stock persists to expiry. Merchant approval plus buyer acceptance transfers the item and splits the price under the profit-cap rule atomically while both are in camps, with distance-independent terminal delivery between camps. No custom prices or arbitrary item gifts in this version. Retail potion/rescue/compass stock lasts three rounds; reinforced lantern five. Self-use stock has already paid wholesale on manufacture; a legal camp withdrawal costs no further coins or action, and using the item later consumes the normal prepared action. No self-transfer revenue. The catalog is selected for the first playtest; its use has not been validated by a combat simulation.

Compass purchases and use require the Explorer career; unsold Merchant compass stock is not usable personally. It is not available as a free basic tool or cache item. The [compass contract](compass-and-natural-exit.md) now uses nine retail/eight wholesale and one prepared single-use Survey action. At depth zero it shows a stable 90° approximate sector toward the nearest of three real scattered openings; every map contains them, so there are no decoy readings. Target unaided natural escapes below 1% in validation, without claiming that rate is already achieved. This replaces the old 10% map gate. No free environmental clue, route counter, special wall or guaranteed two-room failed branch exists. Ordinary exploration without that purchase is pure luck.

The Merchant receives one bound personal potion at arrival, healing one HP for one prepared action and lasting three rounds from issue. It counts toward the fixed seven carried items; at most three unexpired personal grants may exist. If full, a current-round terminal claim waits for camp collection with capacity, then expires if unclaimed. No separate carried potion pouch, sale or coin conversion. See the storage contract above. Camp return grants no other healing.

## Guard protection

A Guard sells at most one wallet lock for one coin and one life shield for two coins each round. The same customer may buy both. Each consumes one Guard kit/action; customer pays no action. Self-hiring is prohibited. The contract is confirmed before second 28, paying the Guard immediately and locking the required kit/action. Delivery at second 30 precedes theft. An incapacitated Guard cannot offer services. Both parties must be in Safe Zones when confirming, but need not share a terminal.

Guard offers must be explicitly authorized during the **current** choices window and bind that round’s available kit/action. A future draft is nonbinding and cannot auto-activate; reject stale-round offers. A normal disconnect after authorization does not erase a valid accepted delivery at second 30. Because authorization is at or after second10 and a later disconnect has110 base seconds of grace, a current-round offer cannot reach permanent timeout before delivery. The earlier report of that payment-to-timeout gap was incorrect under these rules; no new refund exception is required.

Wallet lock blocks all end-choice theft for that round; it does not protect corpses or prevent voluntary spending. Life shield blocks one entire ordinary weapon hit, spending both shield and attacking weapon. It does not prevent trial execution. Services expire at next arrival and survive the Guard's later death. Public accepted contracts show original delivery, not off-screen live shield breaks. No protected escrow or withdrawal exists.

## Combat, death, looting, and ghosts

| Weapon | Price | Damage | Proposed reach | Full-health unshielded hits |
|---|---:|---:|---|---:|
| Club | 4 + S(r) | 1 | 1 tile, same room | 3 |
| Blade | 7 + S(r) | 2 | 1 tile, same room | 2 |
| Single-shot gun | 10 + S(r) | 3 | 4 tiles, same room | 1 |

Selected scarcity surcharge S(r)=floor((r−1)/2), where r is the current round. Prices increase every two rounds:

| Rounds | Club | Blade | Gun | Execution-trial fee |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1–2 | 4 | 7 | 10 | 6 |
| 3–4 | 5 | 8 | 11 | 7 |
| 5–6 | 6 | 9 | 12 | 8 |
| 7–8 | 7 | 10 | 13 | 9 |
| 9–10 | 8 | 11 | 14 | 10 |

Everyone can buy weapons at the private machine. An older stored weapon must pay any increase since its last paid surcharge when equipped at camp. Buying and using it in the same round has no extra fee; re-equipping the same paid weapon that round never charges twice. This prevents cheap stockpiling from bypassing late costs. Its original expiry does not change. Show the exact purchase/top-up quote before acceptance. No extra payment is required in the field, and an unaffordable top-up leaves the weapon unequipped rather than consuming an action. One owned weapon total, one attack per person per round, no mid-field purchases. All weapons break on one valid hit, even blocked. Attacks require a prepared action, range, line of sight, a living target currently visible under server-calculated cone/light rules, and field phase outside Safe Zones. An attack can injure without eliminating. HP persists until healing or death. No critical hits, random accuracy, headshots, or armor penetration. A gun's high damage, reach, and action efficiency make it the ultimate weapon; cheaper weapons can finish wounded targets or spend shields.

Same-tick valid attacks are selected from a pre-attack snapshot so mutual kills are possible. Apply them in rotating seat priority for shield consumption and lethal credit; already accepted attacks still resolve if their attacker dies in that batch. One death transition and one corpse per victim. Witnesses and victim learn an observed attacker; only a neutral elimination notice is global. Hidden purchases and wallets provide no public evidence feed.

Death transfers the remaining wallet into a corpse and destroys inventory, cancels pending work, lowers the group requirement by 11, and leaves prior deposits intact. Loot is a separate prepared action. A non-Scavenger may claim floor(body coins / 2) once per body globally, leaving at least one coin if any existed. If that amount is zero, show the unavailable ordinary claim without spending an action. Further ordinary looters cannot take the remainder. A Scavenger takes all remaining coins with one action whether or not an ordinary claim happened. Chest piles remain fully collectible by any career. No free loot for the killer or vote caller.

The first valid **accepted Loot start** reserves the chest, corpse or cache pickup. Same-tick starts use rotating original-seat priority. A competing request while a valid reservation exists consumes nothing. Scavenger speed does not displace an earlier valid claimant. Release on cancellation, death, invalidation or cutoff; the accepted holder's action/tool remains spent. Completion and release are atomic.

An ordinary corpse claimant takes floor-half once globally for that body, then releases the remainder for a Scavenger. An untouched body can instead be fully claimed by a Scavenger. Chest coins are collected in full; item pickups require capacity at completion and retain their original expiry. A failed completion due to full inventory leaves the pickup and releases its claim. Mining keeps its separate reservation and Worker-priority rules.

An unshielded full-health gun kill costs ten in rounds one/two but fourteen in rounds nine/ten, against an 11-coin fare reduction. The early one-coin saving becomes a late three-coin deficit before actions, shields, loot, or lost labor. This intentionally supersedes the older requirement that a late gun kill always be cheaper than paying one fare; late loot or denial can still motivate killing. Shoot + Loot + Deposit takes three actions; two free actions cannot do all three. Early killing may destroy essential future income. Trial execution costs less money but requires votes. Elimination can still be attractive for loot, denial, or a successful majority vote. Rising prices reduce the automatic late fare-saving incentive but do not establish that all killing is irrational, particularly against an Explorer. This remains a playtest concern.

Ghosts can roam all discovered space through walls and **see every player avatar there**, regardless of darkness, walls or distance: living avatars and other ghosts. They also know public escaped/dead/disconnected roster status. Escaped players have no remaining physical avatar to locate. Ghosts get no unopened geometry, wallets, inventories, secret careers, remote HP, remote action details or other owners' Compass journals. They cannot interact with the economy, open rooms, vote or issue living actions. Roaming ghost avatars are sent only to ghosts, never living players or escaped spectators; an eligible murderer receives the separate private haunt overlay, not a new ability to observe roaming ghosts. Seeing avatars is an explicit exception to living-player LOS filtering, not permission to receive all server state.

Escaped spectators retain a bounded freely movable observer camera in discovered space with local room/light/LOS-filtered live entities, no economy commands and no haunt. Their camera has no public physical avatar. Eliminated players have the ghost exception above. Forfeited seats get neither live observation nor haunt; final results remain separate.

Each ghost may haunt **only their own murderer, once per base round**, at any time while the match is active, including camp phases, trials and Explorer meetings. Newly killed ghosts may use their current round's allowance immediately. No distance/room/LOS requirement. A new base round resets allowance; meeting/reconnect/recovery does not. Normal lethal-hit credit identifies the murderer; a successful trial uses its caller. No killer is invented for a forfeit. The murderer can receive a haunt while alive, a ghost or an authorized escaped spectator.

Persist the per-ghost last-used round and command receipt. Accept only while the murderer has an authorized connected recipient session. A successful committed live delivery spends the allowance even if its acknowledgment is lost; retry returns the receipt without a second effect. Delivery notifications are transient and deduplicated by event ID; receipt/reconnect responses never trigger or replay the animation. Reject an already-offline/ineligible target without spending; never queue an offline scare or replay old transient effects after reconnect. The sender sees only a generic unavailable result. Different ghosts have independent allowances. When simultaneous effects reach one recipient, combine them in one 450 ms appearance strip rather than stacking motion or audio.

The effect is a **450 ms small edge-of-world-viewport pop** of that ghost's recognizable public-career character, visible only to ghost and murderer. Keep names/seat markers and face identity; no Smuggler reveal. No gore, threatening face, full-screen flash, camera shake, loud sting, control lock, vision obstruction or changed deadline/HP/coins. Keep action buttons, votes, clock and targets clear. Reduced motion uses a static brief ghost badge; optional quiet sound follows the player's audio setting. No extra custom text, emotes or timing messages. Haunt timing itself can convey a limited signal, an accepted consequence of the user-selected anytime ability.

## Democratic execution

Selected default: a living player in a Safe Zone can spend 6 + S(r) coins and one prepared Trial action during field time to accuse a distinct other living player in that same Safe Zone; no self-accusation. Maximum one accepted trial per round. The fee burns, is nonrefundable, and does not purchase a guilty verdict. An invalid request costs nothing. Accused is held in the originating Safe Zone for the trial.

The round clock pauses for 30 extra seconds. Normal economic/physical actions and all attacks pause; movement remains allowed. Active channels retain remaining duration only while uninterrupted. Movement during the meeting marks interruption even if the actor returns before resumption. On resume, recheck range/line-of-sight and actor survival; an interrupted or now-invalid channel cancels without refund. Electorate freezes to all living, unescaped players, including accused and caller. A vote can be cast or changed only while the voter is in any discovered Safe Zone. Nominations, proposal approvals, and other planning choices use the same camp-presence rule. Ghosts and departed players cannot vote. Approval requires strictly more than half of the entire frozen electorate, not merely votes cast; abstentions/disconnects therefore cannot enable a minority execution. Ties do nothing. Ballots remain private until aggregate verdict; one vote per person, editable until cutoff.

Trial request ordering: finish the current authoritative field tick (including valid attacks and surviving channel completions), then revalidate queued trial requests against the resulting state. Among valid competing requests, choose one by rotating seat priority, charge only that caller, commit the frozen electorate, and pause subsequent play. Reject the rest without charging or consuming their Trial actions. Recheck that the field phase is still open; a request at or after second 90 cannot extend it. No second trial may start while a trial is active or after that round's trial allowance is spent.

A passed verdict eliminates the accused regardless of HP or life shield and leaves an ordinary corpse. Caller receives execution credit and becomes the ghost's haunt target. Then resume the exact suspended phase time. The consumed caller action and fee are never refunded for an acquittal. This electorate/threshold and forced detention are selected initial defaults needing small-roster social testing.

## Contributions, escape checks, and results

A prepared Deposit action transfers any positive affordable whole-coin amount during field, return, or settlement, from any room, without a coin fee. One donation consumes one action/Deposit marker; two donations consume two. Deposit all means the current wallet at processing time. Trapped players may deposit. A zero/invalid/duplicate request cannot mint money or consume twice. The public fund is irreversible and unstealable; no role can withdraw it.

Numeric proposals specify requested amounts for living players. Approve/reject marks agreement only; everyone still needs their own Deposit actions. Edits, participant changes, and round end invalidate approvals. Values cannot reveal whether another wallet can afford them. Only one active proposal per proposer, with bounded rates.

At each round end, take one frozen snapshot after settlement. **Natural-exit success has priority:** if any one of the scattered hidden openings has been discovered and at least one player remains alive underground, every living unescaped player wins together, including every Smuggler. No 25-coin payment, group fare, or one-Smuggler limit applies to this shared natural escape. Earlier escaped players keep their wins; dead players remain ghosts and are not revived. End the match immediately. Physical travel to the far exit is not an additional requirement: discovery unlocks the common route at the scheduled check.

If no natural exit qualifies, evaluate the paid routes:

1. Let N be all living, unescaped people, including hidden Smugglers. The public route costs 11 × N; this replaces the earlier proposed 12 after the October 28 resource/camp analysis. Death, permanent forfeit, and an earlier private departure reduce future N. Including covers avoids revealing secret role counts through the target.
2. Paid group escape qualifies if N > 0, at least one living ordinary player remains, and the fund covers 11 × N.
3. Each living Smuggler qualifies privately with at least 25 personal coins. At most one can leave at this check. If several qualify, use the round's rotating seat priority; never client request speed. Burn that winner's 25 coins and mark an irreversible private escape.
4. Group and private qualifications use the same snapshot. If the group qualifies, all living ordinary players escape; one qualifying Smuggler may also win at this check. Burn the paid group fare. End the match immediately. Other Smugglers still underground lose. Ordinary players who already died do not receive survival wins.
5. If only a Smuggler departs, everyone else continues. No same-check quota recalculation or second Smuggler departure. Next round uses the reduced N. Earlier private winners keep their wins if the group later succeeds or fails.
6. End immediately if nobody living remains underground, or at round ten after this check. Remaining underground players lose at the deadline. If no ordinary players remain, only the private route can qualify among paid escapes; surplus public funds do not create a Smuggler win. A discovered natural opening still takes priority and can save all remaining Smugglers.

This implements the user's latest answers: simultaneous wins are allowed, one private departure occurs per round, and group escape ends the match. “Immediately” means immediately when the scheduled round-end check processes eligibility, not a mid-round bypass. The later user decision makes natural escape an exception that saves all survivors, including Smugglers, for free. Paid-route tie priority and the fare counting hidden covers are selected initial implementation defaults. Automatic round-end outcomes do not create a field purchase menu; discretionary planning and purchases still require camp.

Rank paid ordinary-group winners by total contributed coins, using shared ranks on ties. On a natural-exit win, rank all surviving shared winners, including Smugglers, by the same contribution rule. Show private escape winners separately rather than mixing their spending into a contribution ranking. Ghosts and other losers receive statistics without a survival award. Show factual highlights from committed events: first mine, trap streak, big chest, shield survival, elimination, rescue, and contributions. No invented funny events. Own theft totals may be shown privately; no public victim–thief associations or theft timing recap. Killer attribution and secret identities may be revealed at final match end. Escaped spectators use the bounded local-LOS observer camera specified above; only eliminated ghosts have global avatar visibility. Neither receives hidden roles, wallets or unopened rooms.
